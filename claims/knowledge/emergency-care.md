# Emergency care and surprise bills
scope: general

## Emergency care is covered at any hospital
In a medical emergency, go to the nearest emergency room. Under the federal No Surprises Act (in effect since January 1, 2022), most health plans must cover emergency services without prior approval and whether or not the hospital or doctor is in network. This applies to PPO, HMO and EPO plans alike.

## What you pay for out-of-network emergency care
For emergency services, your cost sharing (deductible, coinsurance, copays) must be no higher than if the care had been in network, and it counts toward your in-network deductible and out-of-pocket maximum. The out-of-network emergency provider cannot balance bill you for the difference. If an emergency room bill charges you out-of-network rates, it may be wrong and is worth questioning.

## Surprise bills at in-network hospitals
The same law protects you when you choose an in-network hospital or surgery center but are treated there by an out-of-network doctor you did not choose, such as an anesthesiologist, radiologist or assistant surgeon. You should pay only your in-network cost share for those services unless you signed a written notice and consent form in advance.

## What to do if you get a surprise bill
Compare the bill with your explanation of benefits, then contact your insurer and the provider and say the charge is protected under the No Surprises Act. Do not pay the disputed amount while it is being reviewed. You can also file a complaint with the federal No Surprises Help Desk; details are at cms.gov/nosurprises.

## Urgent care is not the same as emergency care
An urgent care clinic treats problems that need same-day care but are not emergencies, such as a sprain or a minor cut. Urgent care visits are not automatically protected like emergency room visits, so an out-of-network urgent care clinic may be covered at out-of-network rates or not at all, depending on your plan type.
