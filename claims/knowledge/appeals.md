# Appeals and claim reviews
scope: general

## Your right to appeal
If your insurer denies a claim or pays less than you expected, you can ask it to reconsider. This is called an internal appeal. Most plans must tell you the reason for the denial and how to appeal in writing, usually on your explanation of benefits.

## Deadline to file an internal appeal
You have 180 days (about six months) from the date you receive the denial notice to file an internal appeal. Send a letter or the insurer's appeal form, a copy of the denial or EOB, the bill, and any supporting notes, for example a doctor's letter explaining why the care was needed.

## How long the insurer has to decide
The insurer must decide an internal appeal within 30 days for care you have not received yet, and within 60 days for care you have already received. For urgent cases the decision is due within 72 hours.

## External review
If the internal appeal is denied, you can ask for an external review by an independent reviewer. You must request it within 4 months after receiving the final internal denial. If the external reviewer rules in your favor, the insurer must pay.

## Billing errors are fixed with the provider, not the insurer
If the problem is a mistake on the bill itself, such as a duplicate charge or a service you never received, contact the provider's billing office first and ask for an itemized bill. An appeal to the insurer is for coverage decisions. ClaimSense can draft an appeal letter for charges you mark for review.
